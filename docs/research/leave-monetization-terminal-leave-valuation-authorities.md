# Official authorities for leave monetization and terminal-leave valuation inputs

**Research question:** Which current official Philippine sources define ordinary
and special monetization and terminal-leave valuation for an ordinary appointed
civilian National Government Agency (NGA) employee? What do they establish about
scope, amendments, effectivity, salary basis, valuation date, credit quantities,
factor, rounding, funding, approvals, forms, certifications, and unresolved
gaps?

**As of:** 2026-09-15 (Philippine Standard Time)

**Source boundary:** This note uses only primary Philippine government sources
(CSC, DBM, and the FY 2026 General Appropriations Act). It records the
authority and evidence a later product decision can rely on; it does not choose
the product workflow or invent a missing national rule. The baseline is the
ordinary civilian NGA profile. Teachers, personnel covered by special leave or
retirement laws, Constitutional Commission members with a special regime, and
other populations with a separate authority profile are not silently folded into
this baseline.

## Resolution

The direct national rule is a leave-credit conversion, not an independently
calculated allowance. Leave supplies the approved, account-owned quantity; the
monetization or terminal-leave amount is based on the authoritative salary and
the national constant factor. The controlling current formula is:

```text
money value = highest monthly salary received × monetized/commutable leave credits × 0.0481927
```

CSC MC No. 2, s. 2016 changed the factor because Republic Act No. 9849 added
Eidul Adha and Eidul Fitr to the regular holidays. DBM Budget Circular No.
2016-2 adopted that factor for both terminal leave benefits (TLB) and
monetization of leave credits (MLC). The source does not prescribe final-peso
rounding, a general salary effective-date rule for MLC, or an allocation/rounding
rule for fractional special-monetization days. Those are genuine unresolved
questions, not safe defaults.

## Direct rules

### Applicability and covered quantities

The Omnibus Rules on Leave are addressed to departments, bureaus, NGAs, LGUs,
GOCCs with original charters, SUCs, and their civil service officials and
employees. Section 22 expressly covers career and non-career personnel who are
permanent, temporary, casual, or coterminous. The same issuance gives teachers
separate vacation-service-credit provisions and excludes a terminal claim where
the leave benefit is covered by special law; those populations need their own
authority profile. [CSC MC No. 41, s. 1998, Rules I and XVI](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/1998/mc41s1998.pdf)

| Case | Direct quantity rule | What is not included by the cited rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary/regular MLC | An eligible employee with at least **15 vacation-leave (VL) days** may monetize a **minimum of 10 days**, must retain **at least 5 VL days**, and may monetize **at most 30 days in a given year**. Section 22 names VL only. | Sick leave (SL), special-leave privileges, and other category-specific leave are not pulled into this rule. |
| Special MLC | **50% or more of accumulated VL/SL credits** may be monetized for valid and justifiable reasons. CSC MC No. 16, s. 2002 gives examples: health/medical/hospital needs; force-majeure calamities and accidents; educational needs; mortgages/loans benefiting the employee or immediate family; extreme financial need; and analogous cases determined by CSC. It requires favorable agency-head recommendation and available funds. | The issuance does not state whether the percentage is applied per account or to a combined balance, nor how fractional days are rounded. |
| Terminal leave | On retirement, voluntary resignation, or separation through no fault, accumulated **VL and/or SL** may be commuted **without a day limit**, regardless of when credits were earned, **exclusive of Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays**. Terminal leave itself earns no further credits. | Special-leave privileges and special-law leave are not ordinary commutable VL/SL credits. |

The five-day retention rule belongs to ordinary Section 22 monetization. It is
not stated as a condition for Section 23 special monetization, which instead
uses the 50%-or-more rule and valid-reason/funding controls. Section 21's
special-leave privileges are non-cumulative and non-commutative, so they are not
ordinary monetization inputs. [CSC MC No. 41, ss. 21-24 and 35-42](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/1998/mc41s1998.pdf)

### Salary basis and factor

CSC MC No. 41's original Section 24 used monthly salary divided by 22 working
days. CSC MC No. 8, s. 2003 reconciled MLC with the terminal-leave formula and
published the then-current `.0478087` factor / `20.916667` equivalent. That
issuance is no longer the current rule: CSC Resolution No. 2100020, circularized
as CSC MC No. 5, s. 2021, expressly repealed MC No. 8, s. 2003 effective 24
April 2021. [CSC MC No. 8, s. 2003](https://csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/2003/mc08s2003.pdf),
[CSC MC No. 5, s. 2021](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/2021/MC05/MC%20No.%2005%2C%20s.%202021.pdf)

CSC MC No. 2, s. 2016 replaced the old constant with:

```text
12 months / [365 days − (104 Saturdays and Sundays + 12 legal holidays)]
= 12 / 249 = 0.0481927
```

It says the circular takes effect 15 days after publication of CSC Resolution
No. 1501530; the resolution was published on 7 January 2016. DBM BC No. 2016-2
adopts `TLB = S × D × CF`, where `S` is the **highest monthly salary received**,
`D` is the number of accumulated VL and SL credits, and `CF` is `0.0481927`.
DBM also states that the equivalent month for this purpose is 20.75 working
days. [CSC MC No. 2, s. 2016](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/2016/MC%20No.%2002%20s.%202016.pdf),
[DBM BC No. 2016-2, secs. 1-3.1](https://www.dbm.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/Issuances/2016/Budget%20Circular/BC%202016%20-%202%20-%20COMPUTATION%20AND%20FUNDING%20OF%20TERMINAL%20LEAVE%20BENEFITS%20AND%20MONETIZATION%20OF%20LEAVE%20CREDITS%20%281%29.pdf)

For terminal leave on retirement or voluntary resignation, Section 39 is more
specific: salary is the highest monthly salary received at any time during the
employee's government service, not merely the latest salary, unless the latest
is the highest. The direct source does not separately define which salary
components count as “monthly salary”; DBM evidence uses the NOSA/NOSI or another
official salary record. [CSC MC No. 41, ss. 35-40](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/1998/mc41s1998.pdf),
[DBM current TLB documentary requirements](https://www.dbm.gov.ph/index.php/central-office/services-for-client-agencies/2500-release-of-obligational-and-or-disbursement-authorities-for-terminal-leave-retirement-gratuity-claims-monetization-of-leave-credits-and-trust-receipts)

### Terminal event and valuation cutoff

Section 35 makes resignation, retirement, or separation through no fault a
condition precedent to filing for terminal leave. Section 36 sends the
commutation application to the department head for approval. CSC MC No. 15, s.
2020 amended Section 38: a terminal-leave payment request may be brought **at
any time after** the official or employee severs the connection with the
employer. The old 10-year claim period must not be used for current claims.
[CSC MC No. 15, s. 2020](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/2020/MC15/MC%20No.%2015%2C%20s.%202020.pdf)

The current DBM release and payment checklists operationalize the balance cutoff
as the last date of service: they request a leave card “as at last date of
service” or an HRMO certificate of leave credits, and an updated service record
including LWOP. For the separate devolved-personnel process, DBM BC No. 2021-1
likewise requires the transferred leave-card balance as of retirement date and
labels the certification “as of Date of Request/Retirement.” This is strong
documentary evidence for a terminal-leave snapshot tied to effective
separation/last service, while the later claim date does not reopen ordinary
credit earning. [DBM TLB payment checklist](https://www.dbm.gov.ph/index.php/central-office/services-for-the-bureau-service-office-employee/3310-processing-of-claims-of-suppliers-service-providers-and-agency-officials-and-employees-for-payment-through-list-of-due-and-demandable-accounts-payable-advice-to-debit-account-lddap-ada),
[DBM BC No. 2021-1](https://www.dbm.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/Issuances/2021/Budget-Circular/BUDGET-CIRCULAR-NO-2021-1.pdf)

The national sources do not state an equivalent single valuation date for
ordinary or special MLC. Form 6 has a leave-credit certification “as of” a date,
but the sources do not say whether MLC salary and balance are fixed at request,
approval, fund release, or payment. The published formula's “highest monthly
salary received” also does not resolve that timing for MLC.

### Forms and certifications

CSC MC No. 5, s. 2021 prescribed the revised **CS Form No. 6, Revised 2020**
and discontinued the old CS Form No. 41. The form has fields for:

- employee salary and application date;
- “Monetization of Leave Credits” and “Terminal Leave” under the other-purpose
  choices;
- number of working days and inclusive dates;
- commutation requested/not requested;
- a VL/SL certification as of a stated date showing total earned, less this
  application, and balance;
- recommendation for approval/disapproval; and
- approved days with pay, without pay, or other treatment.

[CS Form No. 6, Revised 2020 (official CSC download)](https://www.csc.gov.ph/downloads/memorandum-circulars/category/298-mc-no-05-s-2021-amendment-to-omnibus-rules-on-leave-csc-mc-no-41-s-1998-as-amended?download=2331%3Acs-form-no-6-revised-2020-application-for-leave-secured)

For DBM release authority, the current Table A2 lists the following evidence:

- **TLB:** special budget request; list of retirees to be paid; completed Form 6;
  certified updated service record including LWOP; HRMO-certified statement of
  leave credits with balance and amount computation; latest NOSA/NOSI; and,
  where applicable, a name-change certificate or teaching designation order.
- **MLC:** special budget request; list of employees with approved MLC
  applications; completed Form 6 Revised 2020; latest NOSA/NOSI or service
  record; and other pertinent authority documents.

[DBM Table A2 — release of TLB, retirement gratuity, MLC, and trust-receipt
  authorities](https://www.dbm.gov.ph/index.php/central-office/services-for-client-agencies/2500-release-of-obligational-and-or-disbursement-authorities-for-terminal-leave-retirement-gratuity-claims-monetization-of-leave-credits-and-trust-receipts)

For payment claims, the current DBM checklist additionally calls for the
accepted resignation or retirement letter, leave card/certificate as of the
last service date, clearance from money/property/legal accountability, complete
service record, SALN, and an appointment/NOSA showing the highest salary when
the last appointment is not the highest. It also lists an accountant-certified
computation and approval documents. These are current government payment
controls, not a universal claim that every agency must expose the same employee
checklist. [DBM claims checklist, Terminal Leave Benefits](https://www.dbm.gov.ph/index.php/central-office/services-for-the-bureau-service-office-employee/3310-processing-of-claims-of-suppliers-service-providers-and-agency-officials-and-employees-for-payment-through-list-of-due-and-demandable-accounts-payable-advice-to-debit-account-lddap-ada)

For the DBM regional office's own personnel, the current official checklist
requires an approved 10-day application with HR certification of leave balance,
Head-of-Agency approval for a request covering more than 10 days, and—at 50%
or more—support such as a medical clinical abstract or barangay certification
for calamity-related financial need. This is useful evidence of the source
documents expected by a budget/accounting reviewer, but the page identifies it
as a DBM Regional Office service and should not be mistaken for a universal
national form. [DBM regional monetization checklist](https://www.dbm.gov.ph/index.php/regional-offices/services-for-the-bureau-service-office-employee/3934-processing-of-request-for-cash-advances-and-claims-for-monetization-of-leave-credits)

### Funding and approval evidence

DBM BC No. 2016-2 assigns funding as follows:

- compulsory NGA retirees' TLB is initially charged to the agency's built-in
  lumpsum, with deficiencies from the Pension and Gratuity Fund (PGF);
- optional NGA retirees' TLB, qualifying financially unable GOCCs, and devolved
  personnel are charged against PGF; and
- MLC of National Government personnel and transferred leave credits of NGA
  personnel devolved to LGUs are charged against PGF.

The circular makes the head of entity personally liable for payments not made
under the circular, without prejudice to refund of excess payment. [DBM BC No.
2016-2, secs. 3.2-5](https://www.dbm.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/Issuances/2016/Budget%20Circular/BC%202016%20-%202%20-%20COMPUTATION%20AND%20FUNDING%20OF%20TERMINAL%20LEAVE%20BENEFITS%20AND%20MONETIZATION%20OF%20LEAVE%20CREDITS%20%281%29.pdf)

The current FY 2026 GAA continues the authority: PGF Special Provision 1(d)
covers MLC of National Government personnel and transferred credits of NGA
personnel devolved to LGUs; 1(e) covers deficiencies in authorized retirement
and terminal-leave benefits; and provision 3 directs release to agencies (with
GOCC/LGU handling through the Bureau of the Treasury). The FY 2026 table
appropriates a distinct amount for MLC, but annual amounts and priorities are
budget-year facts. [FY 2026 GAA, Pension and Gratuity Fund, Special Provisions
1(d)-1(e) and 3](https://www.dbm.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/GAA/GAA2026/VolumeIB/PGF.pdf)

DBM Circular Letter No. 2024-1 demonstrates why funding evidence must be
effective-dated. For FY 2024 it limited MLC payment to extraordinary
circumstances (for example, medical emergencies and calamity concerns), required
available agency PS allotments after RG/TL needs, prohibited charging RG/TL
appropriations for MLC, and allowed PGF charging only when agency PS was
insufficient. That circular took effect immediately but is expressly FY2024;
the official DBM circular-letter index does not show a later MLC-specific annual
letter. Do not carry its FY2024 priorities into a later year without a current
GAA/DBM issuance. [DBM Circular Letter No. 2024-1](https://www.dbm.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/Issuances/2024/Circular-Letter/CIRCULAR-LETTER-NO-2024-1.pdf),
[DBM Circular Letters index](https://www.dbm.gov.ph/index.php/circular-letters)

Approval evidence differs by case. Section 36 requires department-head approval
for terminal commutation. CSC MC No. 16 requires favorable agency-head
recommendation for special MLC, and the DBM checklist requires Head-of-Agency
approval for a request exceeding 10 days. Form 6 separately records the
authorized officer's balance certification, recommendation, and approving
official. A mere employee request is not the funding or approval evidence.

## Effectivity and amendment chain

1. **CSC MC No. 41, s. 1998** (24 December 1998) supplied the ordinary VL/SL,
   Sections 22-24 monetization, and Sections 35-43 terminal-leave baseline.
2. **CSC MC No. 16, s. 2002** was published 7 June 2002 and took effect 22 June
   2002. It amended Section 23 from “50%” to “50% or more,” listed valid and
   justifiable reasons, and required favorable agency-head recommendation and
   funds.
3. **CSC MC No. 8, s. 2003** adopted the then-terminal factor for MLC, but its
   provisions were expressly repealed by CSC Resolution No. 2100020 / **CSC MC
   No. 5, s. 2021**, effective 24 April 2021.
4. **CSC MC No. 2, s. 2016** changed the constant to `0.0481927`; **DBM BC No.
   2016-2** adopted it for TLB and MLC and states effectivity on 23 January 2016
   for employees retiring after 22 January 2016. The sources do not give a
   separate MLC request/payment cut-off date.
5. **CSC MC No. 15, s. 2020** changed terminal-claim timing from the former
   10-year period to any time after severance.
6. **CSC MC No. 5, s. 2021** prescribed the Revised 2020 Form 6 and discontinued
   Form 41; the temporary old-form transition ended 31 December 2021 under CSC
   MC No. 8, s. 2021.

The old `.0478087` factor in pre-2016 DBM/CSC pages is stale for the ordinary
current baseline. The source chain above is the reason a rule record must carry
issuer, version, effectivity, and supersession rather than relying on an
issuance date alone.

## Rounding and unresolved gaps

The controlling sources publish the seven-decimal factor `0.0481927` and the
equivalent 20.75 working-day month. They do **not** prescribe:

- rounding the exact `12/249` ratio versus using the published decimal;
- rounding fractional leave credits, especially for the 50%-or-more case;
- rounding the final peso amount to centavos or at what calculation stage;
- whether special monetization's percentage is calculated against a combined
  VL+SL balance or independently per account;
- the valuation date for ordinary/special MLC salary and balance (request,
  approval, fund release, or payment);
- a universal annual MLC application window or priority order;
- a separate national computation form beyond Form 6 and agency/DBM schedules;
- the precise salary components included in “monthly salary” for the factor; or
- a single current rule for involuntary/no-fault separation where Section 39's
  explicit highest-salary wording names retirement and voluntary resignation.

The direct terminal evidence points to an effective-separation/last-service
balance cutoff and historical highest salary, but it does not prescribe a
general correction/successor-snapshot rule for backdated leave or separation
changes. Current annual GAA and DBM release instructions also need to be
revalidated before treating a funding source or priority as permanent.
