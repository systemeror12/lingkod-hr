# Official authorities for overtime, COC, and CTO

**Research question:** Which current national authorities govern overtime, Compensatory Overtime Credit (COC), and Compensatory Time-Off (CTO) for civilian Appointed Personnel in a National Government Agency (NGA)? Which points do they hand to the agency, and which to an official deciding each case?

**As of:** 2026-10-25 (Philippine Standard Time)

**Scope:** The Reference Agency's civilian NGA Appointed Personnel, as in [native-payroll-component-authorities.md](native-payroll-component-authorities.md). Job Order and Contract of Service workers, GOCCs, LGUs, and special-law populations need their own applicability decision. Overtime stacked with night-shift differential was not examined. This record states what the sources say. Product behaviour belongs to [#317][i317] (overtime authority, service, and compensation), [#318][i318] (COC ledger and CTO), and [#347][i347] (overtime pay).

## Marks

Each point carries exactly one mark:

- **National**: a national source states the rule and its value.
- **Agency-configured**: a national source expressly hands this point to the agency as a standing value or procedure. The row names the evidence the agency must produce. A general power to issue internal rules does not count.
- **Per-case decision**: a national source requires an authorized official to decide each case. The row names who decides and what.
- **No authority found**: no source located states the rule. The point blocks until an authority is produced or the named ticket records a decision.

Rules come from circulars, GAA provisions, and statutes. CSC opinions, COA decisions, and court decisions belong in the Interpretation column; a dash there means none was located. Lines marked _Reading_ are this research's reading of a source, not an authority. HR blogs, agency FAQs, and CSC Forum answers are not cited.

## Decision

- Overtime needs authority from the Head of Office or agency head for each case: who works, when, and for what. No source located allows crediting or paying service rendered without it.
- CTO is the general rule and cash Overtime Pay the exception. The agency head chooses the manner of compensation on each overtime authority; it is not a standing agency setting.
- These are national: the COC ratios (1.0 and 1.5), the 40-hour monthly cap, the 120-hour cap on the unexpended balance, validity through the year after the year earned, CTO blocks of 4 or 8 hours, and the five-consecutive-day limit. The one express hand-off to the agency is the CTO block size under an alternative work schedule.
- COC is forfeited on separation and does not move with the employee to another agency. A promotion keeps it unless the new position is ineligible for overtime. No source addresses leave without pay.
- CTO is time off with full pay and benefits. COCs count as official time for PERA, Additional Compensation, year-end benefits, leave earning, and step increments.
- Under a flexible work arrangement, overtime earns COC or pay only when the employee physically reported for work. Work from home and work from another fixed place earn neither.

## Definitions in the sources

Quoted verbatim.

- **Compensatory Overtime Credit (COC):** "Compensatory Overtime Credit (COC) refers to the accrued number of hours an employee earns as a result of services rendered beyond regular working hours, and/or those rendered on Saturdays, Sundays, Holidays or scheduled days off without the benefit of overtime pay." ([JC 2, s. 2004][jc2004], item 4.0)
- **Compensatory Time-Off (CTO):** "Compensatory Time-Off (CTO) refers to the number of hours or days an employee is excused from reporting for work with full pay and benefits. It is a non-monetary benefit provided to an employee in lieu of overtime pay." ([JC 2, s. 2004][jc2004], item 4.0)
- **CTO, as restated in 2015:** "As defined in item 4.0 of the CSC-DBM Joint Circular No. 2, s. 2004, Compensatory Time-Off or CTO refers to the accrued number of hours an employee earns as a result of services rendered beyond regular working hours, and/or those rendered on Saturdays, Sundays, holidays or scheduled days off without the benefit of Overtime Pay." ([JC 2, s. 2015][jc2015], footnote 3; see [Conflicts](#conflicts))
- **Overtime Pay:** "As defined in the Congress Joint Resolution No. 4. s. 2009, Overtime Pay is the cash payment for work performed by an employee beyond the regular working hours in a day, and those performed on rest days, holidays, and non-working days." ([JC 2, s. 2015][jc2015], footnote 4)
- **Period of overtime service:** "The period of overtime service in a workday for a full-time employee shall include: 8.2.1 Those rendered beyond the normal 8 work hours on scheduled workdays or 40 hours a week, and those rendered on rest days or scheduled days off, holidays, and special non-working days, both exclusive of time for lunch and rest; 8.2.2 Those rendered beyond the prescribed work hours in a shift of 8 hours or more, such as in government hospitals, on scheduled workdays, and those rendered on rest days or scheduled days off, holidays, and special non-working days." ([JC 2, s. 2015][jc2015], item 8.2; item 8.2.3 applies the same periods to drivers and immediate staff of officials.)
- **Regular working hours:** "officers and employees of departments and agencies shall render not less than 8 hours of work a day for 5 days a week or a total of 40 hours a week, exclusive of time for lunch. Generally, such hours shall be from 8:00 in the morning to 12:00 noon and from 1:00 to 5:00 in the afternoon, except Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays." ([JC 2, s. 2015][jc2015], item 8.1, restating Omnibus Rules, Rule XVII, sec. 5)
- **CWW non-working day:** "Monday to Thursday as workdays and Friday as the non-working day" and "any combination of four (4) workdays and one (1) non-working day". ([CSC MC No. 6, s. 2026][mc6-2026], item 2.2)
- **Rest day, scheduled day off, holiday, special non-working day:** the overtime sources use these terms without defining them. The annual holiday proclamation classifies dates; see [attendance-schedule-authorities.md](attendance-schedule-authorities.md#rest-days-holidays-and-work-suspensions).

_Readings behind the interpretation cells:_

- A CWW non-working day is a scheduled day off. In LingkodHR it is a Day Rule rest day, so the two rows agree.
- A special non-working day and a proclaimed local holiday are holidays for item 5.4.2 of JC 2, s. 2004. A `LOCAL_HOLIDAY` resting on an issuance other than a proclamation is a gap.

The source-stated workday row uses LingkodHR's expected duty. Two schedule points are unsettled:

- Read literally, "beyond the normal 8 work hours on scheduled workdays" would make hours 9–10 of a 10-hour CWW day overtime, even though they are expected duty. No source settles this (gap, #317).
- [CSC MC No. 6, s. 2026][mc6-2026] says a holiday or suspension on the CWW non-working day reverts that week to 8-hour workdays (item 2.3). One falling on a CWW workday counts the 10-hour day as complied with, except for skeleton forces of essential 24/7 frontline services (item 2.4). Hours already rendered are not recomputed (item 2.5). _Reading:_ overtime that week is measured against the reverted or deemed schedule.

## Conflicts

1. **Caps and validity.** [CSC MC No. 11, s. 2024][mc11-2024] states: "COCs may accrue up to a maximum of 40 hours per month, with an overall cap of 120 hours within a calendar year. These 120 hours represent the total allowable COCs that an employee can accumulate annually, and they must be utilized within the same year they are earned." JC 2, s. 2004 item 5.5.1 caps the unexpended balance, not annual accrual. JC 2-A item 2.0 extends validity to the immediately succeeding year. _Reading:_ MC No. 11 is a one-event addendum that acts "in alignment with" JC 2, s. 2004. It neither cites nor amends JC 2-A, so the joint circulars remain the rule both issuers signed. Under JC 2-A item 3.0, the CSC resolves accrual and availment issues; no CSC resolution on this conflict was located (#318).
2. **CTO definition.** JC 2, s. 2015 footnote 3 attaches JC 2, s. 2004's COC definition to CTO. JC 2, s. 2004 item 4.0 defines the two terms separately, and the 2015 body defers to the 2004 and 2005 circulars for CTO (item 3.2). The two-term reading matches the joint circulars' operative text (#317, #318).
