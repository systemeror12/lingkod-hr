# Authoritative civilian NGA leave-credit and mandatory-leave rules

**Research question:** What current official national rules govern leave-credit earning, opening balances, requested-unit calculation, deductions, restorations, adjustments, forfeiture or expiration, as-of balances, mandatory or forced leave, and forms for civilian Philippine National Government Agency (NGA) personnel? Which rules are direct, which are interpretation, which conflict, and which details remain agency configuration?

**As of:** 2026-11-14 (Philippine Standard Time). The non-480-minute answers for #654 were checked against the primary texts on this date.

## Decision rule

Use the national rule as the source of truth when an issuance states the entitlement, unit, eligibility, earning, deduction, restoration, or filing requirement. Treat a payroll or HRIS behavior as an implementation interpretation only when the source does not prescribe the data or workflow. Keep special-law regimes and agency choices separate from the ordinary civilian NGA rule. Do not turn a conflict between a statute and an implementing rule into an automatic cash benefit or an automatic deduction.

## Resolution

For an ordinary appointed civilian NGA employee, the governing baseline remains the [CSC Omnibus Rules on Leave, CSC Memorandum Circular (MC) No. 41, s. 1998](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/1998/mc41s1998.pdf), as amended. It gives an eligible employee 15 days of vacation leave (VL) and 15 days of sick leave (SL) per year, with no cap on accumulation, and earns one day of each for every 24 days of actual service. The same issuance governs the working-day charge, paid and unpaid service, leave without pay (LWOP), mandatory or forced leave, monetization, terminal leave, transfer of credits, approvals, and the circumstances in which credits are restored or forfeited.

The baseline is not the whole national leave catalogue. The current rule set also includes maternity, paternity, adoption, solo-parent, violence-against-women-and-children (VAWC), special leave benefits for women, study, rehabilitation, special emergency, and wellness leave. Those benefits have their own eligibility, units, evidence, and cumulative or noncumulative treatment. [CSC MC No. 5, s. 2021](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/2021/MC05/MC%20No.%2005%2C%20s.%202021.pdf) superseded the old maternity and paternity provisions in MC41 and prescribed the current [CS Form No. 6 Revised 2020](https://www.csc.gov.ph/downloads/memorandum-circulars/category/298-mc-no-05-s-2021-amendment-to-omnibus-rules-on-leave-csc-mc-no-41-s-1998-as-amended?download=2331%3Acs-form-no-6-revised-2020-application-for-leave-secured) and CS Form No. 6a. The current form is the national evidence and balance statement for an application, but the national sources do not prescribe a universal historical opening-balance migration algorithm.

### Scope and coverage

MC41 applies to heads and personnel of departments, bureaus, and agencies of the national government, as well as the other listed civil-service institutions. Its ordinary VL and SL entitlement covers appointive permanent, temporary, and casual employees who render the prescribed office hours. A part-time employee earns proportionately. A contractual employee is not ordinarily entitled to VL or SL as a matter of right when the contract carries the 20% premium; if the premium is not given, MC41 says the employee should receive VL and SL. Teachers and people covered by a special leave law follow their special rules, not an assumed ordinary-NGA profile. [MC41, Rule XVI, sections 1-10](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/1998/mc41s1998.pdf).

The amended rule set uses a broad government-employee definition for the maternity, paternity, and adoption rules. It covers appointment or election in NGAs, local governments, GOCCs with original charters, and state or local universities and colleges, with employment statuses including permanent, temporary, provisional, coterminous, fixed-term, substitute, casual, and contractual. That broad category-specific coverage does not erase MC41's separate exclusion for teachers and personnel governed by another special leave law. [MC5, s. 2021, Rule I and sections 11-20](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/2021/MC05/MC%20No.%2005%2C%20s.%202021.pdf).

### MC41 section 25 text

Transcribed from page 13 of the official CSC scan of [MC41](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/1998/mc41s1998.pdf). The PDF has no text layer.

> **Sec. 25. Five days forced/mandatory leave.** - All officials and employees with 10 days or more vacation leave credits shall be required to go on vacation leave whether continuous or intermittent for a minimum of five (5) working days annually under the following conditions:
>
> (a) The head of agency shall, upon prior consultation with the employees, prepare a staggered schedule of the mandatory five-day vacation leave of officials and employees, provided that he may, in the exigency of the service, cancel any previously scheduled leave.
>
> (b) The mandatory annual five-day vacation leave shall be forfeited if not taken during the year. However, in cases where the scheduled leave has been cancelled in the exigency of the service by the head of the agency, the scheduled leave not enjoyed shall no longer be deducted from the total accumulated vacation leave.
>
> (c) Retirement and resignation from the service in a particular year without completing the calendar year do not warrant forfeiture of the corresponding leave credits if concerned employees opted not to avail of the required five-day mandatory vacation leave.
>
> (d) Those with accumulated vacation leave of less than ten (10) days shall have the option to go on forced leave or not. However, officials and employees with accumulated vacation leave of 15 days who availed of monetization for 10 days, under Section 22 hereof, shall still be required to go on forced leave.

Both exceptions, (b) and (c), only prevent forfeiture of untaken days. Neither reduces the five-day obligation. The section does not say when the ten-day threshold is measured or when forfeiture takes effect.

## Credit ledger, units, and balance calculation

### Earning and opening balances

The direct ordinary ledger rule is simple: add one VL and one SL for each 24 days of actual service. Approved leave with pay counts as actual service. LWOP for a reason other than illness does not, and the MC41 tables govern the reduced or absent accrual during authorized LWOP. An employee on a regular schedule earns by the prescribed service period; the source does not authorize a second, hidden accrual rule based on an HRIS posting date.

MC41 defines actual service as continuous service from appointment, including approved leave with pay. For daily-wage employees, the relevant Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays count when the employee is not required to work. Fractions of a day are treated as half or full day under the section 28 threshold. Off-duty days under an irregular schedule are not charged. Flexible schedules charge only the minimum daily hours not served, proportionately. Tardiness or undertime is charged to VL, not SL, except for a health reason supported by a medical certificate and leave application. [MC41, sections 27-34](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/1998/mc41s1998.pdf).

The national sources establish no single universal opening-balance import format or opening-balance cut-off for every agency. They do establish that ordinary VL and SL can accumulate without a cap, that transfer can carry credits under section 47, and that the Form 6 balance certificate reports total earned, less the application, and balance as of the stated date. Therefore an agency must establish the source record, effective date, transfer or appointment evidence, and correction authority for an opening balance. That is an implementation control inferred from the need to apply the direct rules. It is not permission to invent a national reset or expiration rule.

For a transferred employee, MC41 permits transfer or commutation of credits. Transfer is a right where there is no service gap, with the limited one-month gap rule in section 47. The employee must exercise the option within one year of transfer. Military-to-civilian transfer is excluded. If transfer is denied, the office where the credits were earned handles the money claim. [MC41, sections 47-48](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/1998/mc41s1998.pdf).

### Requested-unit calculation

For ordinary VL and SL, MC41 states the unit in days. The entitlement is 15 days each of VL and SL a year, exclusive of Saturdays, Sundays, and public holidays ([MC41, Rule XVI, section 1](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/1998/mc41s1998.pdf)). For an irregular schedule, off-duty days that fall on a Saturday, Sunday, or holiday are excluded from the leave deduction (section 29). A flexible-hours absence is charged in proportion to the hours required for a day's work (section 30). A full day on a four-workday compressed week has its own stated unit, recorded in the non-480-minute section below. Sections 51 to 54 set filing rules only: a VL application for one full day or more goes on the prescribed form, five days in advance whenever possible, and an SL application for one full day or more is filed immediately on return, with advance filing allowed for examination, operation, or recuperation with a medical certificate. They state no requested-unit formula, and this doc does not infer one. The prescribed form records inclusive dates, the number of working days, the commutation choice, and the balance certification.

Other categories do not reuse the ordinary ledger unit:

- Maternity is 105 calendar days for live childbirth or 60 calendar days for miscarriage or emergency termination, plus 15 calendar days for a qualified solo parent.
- Paternity is seven days with full pay for the qualifying first four deliveries.
- Adoption is 60 days, continuous and uninterrupted, under MC5.
- Solo-parent parental leave is not more than seven working days each year.
- Mandatory or forced leave is five working days from the VL account, continuous or intermittent.
- Special emergency leave is up to five days, straight or staggered, and is outside the ordinary credits.
- VAWC leave is up to 10 days and may be continuous or intermittent.
- Wellness leave is up to five agency-granted days and is outside ordinary VL and SL.
- Rehabilitation leave and special leave benefits for women use months, not ordinary VL or SL credits.

The form must preserve the unit stated by the source. Converting a calendar-day statutory benefit to an ordinary working-day debit, or converting an outside-the-ledger privilege into ordinary VL, is an interpretation that the cited rules do not authorize.

### Deductions, restorations, and adjustments

Direct deductions include approved VL and SL, proportional flexible-schedule absences, and VL deductions for tardiness or undertime. When SL is exhausted, VL may be used in its place, but SL may not be used in place of VL. An absence beyond earned VL and SL is LWOP. Unauthorized absence receives no salary and is not deducted from earned credits. A pending formal administrative charge blocks VL with pay under section 61. [MC41, sections 50, 56, and 61](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/1998/mc41s1998.pdf).

The following restorations or non-deductions are direct rules:

- Special emergency or calamity leave is outside the VL and SL accounts.
- Rehabilitation leave is not charged to VL or SL.
- When an employee is exonerated and reinstated, MC41 restores the leave credits covering the period out of service.
- When a scheduled mandatory leave is cancelled for exigency, the unused days are not deducted from accumulated VL.
- MC5's maternity transition applies the RA11210 benefit to covered events from 2019-03-11. A balance correction may therefore be required when an old rule had charged credits for that event.
- A seconded employee on LWOP from the mother agency earns leave in the receiving agency, and the receiving agency handles later commutation or payment.
- A person who is reemployed before the expiration of a commuted leave does not refund the commuted amount but starts with a zero leave balance.

These rules support an auditable adjustment entry tied to the legal event, source document, effective date, approving authority, and before-and-after balance. They do not create a general free-form adjustment right. A correction must be traceable to an applicable rule or an authorized agency decision.

The CS Form 6 certification gives the operational as-of pattern: it asks for the date, total VL or SL earned, less the current application, and resulting balance. That is a direct form requirement. It does not define the agency's accounting cut-off, historical reconstruction method, or approval of a disputed opening balance. Those remain agency configuration and record-governance decisions.

- whether a salary deduction for tardiness or undertime beyond vacation leave credits uses the same day fraction. Section 56 makes the excess without pay; the salary formula is Payroll's (#341).

**The full-day figure and the deficit rule have to agree.** A full CWW day of absence is 1.25 credit days, so a rule that charged 600 late or undertime minutes as one full credit day would contradict §V.B.2.6. The figure matches Table II's own hourly rate carried past eight hours: 0.125 day for each hour, times ten hours, is 1.250. The only linear reading that agrees with both the 1.25 figure and Table II is 480 minutes per credit day. No primary text states that for late or undertime minutes, so this research does not adopt it. It is a decision for the developer, or a CSC clarification.

**Section 28 does not reach these facts.** Section 28's threshold, under which a fraction of one-fourth up to three-fourths counts as one-half day and three-fourths or more counts as one full day, is stated "for purposes of granting leave of absence," within the definition of actual service. No text applies it to Table II fractions, and ADR 0012 keeps exact fractions.

**Good-faith practice before the 2026 resolution is protected.** MC 6 s. 2026 says that agencies and employees who, in good faith and before CSC Resolution No. 2600838, "adopted or implemented alternative interpretations or practices consistent with existing agency guidelines and prevailing understanding of the rules shall not be administratively prejudiced thereby." A rerun must not reopen such a practice without a decision.

**CSC MC No. 1, s. 2025 is not relied on.** OP MC No. 114 §3 and the quoted text of MC 6 s. 2026 cite it. Its text was not located, so nothing here depends on it. MC 6 s. 2026 restates all of Policies §V.B.2 (2.1 through 2.6), which limits what an earlier change to that section could still do. Press reports of the July 2026 revision are secondary and were not used.

Blocking items for #654: (1) late and undertime minutes on a 600-minute compressed-week day; (2) compressed-week Work Dates before 2026-03-06; (3) a holiday or suspension inside a compressed week for agencies with 24/7 skeleton frontline service; (4) part-time days; (5) flexible-hours TARDINESS and UNDERTIME facts; and (6) any other expected duty.

### Forfeiture, expiration, and terminal events

Ordinary VL and SL do not expire and have no accumulation cap. They become commutable on the qualifying retirement, resignation, or separation event, subject to the rule on separation for cause. Separation for cause forfeits credits. Terminal leave itself does not earn additional credits, and an extension beyond the mandatory retirement age does not earn additional credits. [MC41, sections 26 and 35-43](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/1998/mc41s1998.pdf).

> The accepted decision in #205 qualifies the forfeiture reading: CSC MC No. 14, s. 1999, section 65 and the 2025 RACCS, section 73 establish that dismissal does not bar terminal-leave benefits, and the 2025 RACCS, section 135 says dropping from rolls forfeits none. The terminal-leave qualification map follows those authorities.

The following benefits do expire, forfeit, or remain noncumulative under their direct sources:

- The five mandatory VL days are forfeited if not used in the year, except for leave the agency head cancelled for exigency and for retirement or resignation before the end of the calendar year. [MC41, section 25(b) and (c)](#mc41-section-25-text).
- MC41 special leave privileges are noncumulative and noncommutative.
- Paternity, VAWC, special emergency, and wellness leave are noncumulative or noncommutative under their respective rules.
- Solo-parent parental leave is expressly forfeitable and noncumulative under RA11861 and its revised IRR.
- A category-specific leave cannot be silently converted into ordinary VL or SL just because it was not used.

The terminal-leave claim period changed in 2020. The current direct rule is that a request for terminal-leave payment may be brought at any time after the official or employee severs the connection with the employer. [CSC MC No. 15, s. 2020](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/2020/MC15/MC%20No.%2015%2C%20s.%202020.pdf).

## Forms and evidence

The prescribed application is [CS Form No. 6 Revised 2020](https://www.csc.gov.ph/downloads/memorandum-circulars/category/298-mc-no-05-s-2021-amendment-to-omnibus-rules-on-leave-csc-mc-no-41-s-1998-as-amended?download=2331%3Acs-form-no-6-revised-2020-application-for-leave-secured), filed in duplicate with supporting documents. It lists VL, mandatory or forced leave, SL, maternity, paternity, special privilege, solo parent, study, VAWC, rehabilitation, special leave benefits for women, special emergency or calamity, adoption, and other categories. It asks for inclusive dates, number of working days, commutation choice, and the VL and SL balance certification. CS Form No. 6a supports allocation of maternity leave.

MC5 discontinued CS Form 41 for the amended maternity, paternity, and adoption process. [CSC MC No. 8, s. 2021](https://www.csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/2021/MC%20No.%2008%2C%20s.%202021.pdf) allowed agencies that were updating their HRIS or using existing printed forms to use the old form only through 2021-12-31. As of this research date, the transition period has ended.

The form instructions supply category evidence requirements, including medical certificates for the relevant illness or procedure, pregnancy or birth evidence, protection or police evidence for VAWC, placement documents for adoption, disaster evidence for special emergency leave, and a clearance for leave of at least 30 calendar days and terminal leave. A medical certificate must include the physician's identifying and license details under MC5. The agency may validate evidence and protect confidential medical, family, and violence-related information, but it should not add a new entitlement or remove a national one.

## Effectivity, amendments, and source conflicts

The source chain matters because older copies contain different entitlements or forms:

1. MC41, s. 1998, adopted the amended Rule I and Rule XVI baseline. Its later amendments include MC14, s. 1999 and the leave-specific issuances listed in the CSC's official [2025 Memorandum Circular index](https://csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/2025/2025%20CSC%20Memorandum%20Circulars.pdf).
2. MC2, s. 2016 changed the terminal-leave and monetization factor to 0.0481927.
3. MC15, s. 2020 removed the former terminal-leave claim time limit.
4. MC5, s. 2021 amended the maternity, paternity, and adoption provisions and took effect on 2021-04-24. Its maternity amendment is applied to covered events from 2019-03-11 because of RA11210.
5. MC8, s. 2021 ended the old-form transition on 2021-12-31.
6. RA11861 lapsed into law on 2022-06-04 and its section 31 makes it effective 15 days after publication. Its revised IRR is the 2022-09-29 Official Gazette issuance linked below.
7. CSC Resolution No. 2101012 amended adoption-leave application documents and became effective after publication in February 2022.
8. CSC MC1, s. 2026 introduced the discretionary wellness-leave policy, effective 2026-01-01.
9. [OP MC No. 114, s. 2026](https://lawphil.net/executive/mc/mc2026/pdf/mc_114_2026.pdf), dated 6 March 2026, directs agencies to adopt a four-day onsite arrangement "through a compressed workweek or a designated common day for work-from-home arrangements," from 9 March 2026 until lifted or revoked (§3).
10. [CSC MC No. 6, s. 2026](https://csc.gov.ph/phocadownload/userupload/irmo/mc/2026/MC%20No.%2006%20s.%202026%20-%20Amendment%20to%20the%20Policies%20on%20Flexible%20Work%20Arrangements%20in%20the%20Government%20CSC%20Resolutions%20No.%202200209.pdf) amends the compressed-workweek policies (CSC Resolution No. 2200209, Policies §V.B.2) and implements CSC Resolution No. 2600838 of 18 June 2026. It was signed on 24 June 2026, published on 14 August 2026, and takes effect retroactively on 6 March 2026.

The main unresolved conflict is solo-parent leave. RA11861 section 8 calls the seven-day benefit “forfeitable.” The revised IRR repeats “noncumulative and forfeitable” in its parental-leave rule, but its next provision, section 24, is headed “Non-conversion of Parental Leave” and says that unavailed leave “shall be convertible to cash” unless previously agreed otherwise. Those statements cannot all be applied at once. The statute and the IRR's forfeiture sentence support no cash conversion. Until an authoritative correction or controlling interpretation resolves the inconsistency, the safe rule is to record the leave as forfeitable and not create an automatic cash liability. A greater benefit under an applicable law, CBA, or existing agency benefit can still control under the IRR.

A smaller source conflict is nomenclature: the Form 6 checklist says “10-Day VAWC Leave (RA9262/CSC MC15 s. 2005),” while the official CSC issuance found for the rule is MC15, s. 2006. The entitlement and documents should follow the official MC15, s. 2006 text, not the stale form label.

The old MC41 maternity and paternity sections, the old MC8, s. 2003 monetization text, and the old CS Form 41 must not be used when they conflict with RA11210, RA8187 as amended, MC5, or the revised forms. Special-law personnel, teachers, contractual workers covered by a different premium rule, and consultants or job-order workers must be routed to their own authority profile instead of being forced into the ordinary NGA ledger.

## Agency configuration boundary

The national rules leave these implementation choices to the agency, provided they do not reduce a direct entitlement:

- which appointment, status, schedule, and service records establish ordinary coverage and actual service;
- the opening-balance evidence, as-of cut-off, transfer certificate, and correction approval;
- the calendar, shift, holiday, and partial-day calculation configuration;
- the mandatory-leave consultation, staggered schedule, exigency cancellation, and retirement or resignation workflow;
- approval routing, filing lead times, substitute or acting-officer authority, and deemed-approval handling;
- the evidence checklist and retention or confidentiality controls for each category;
- study-leave selection, rehabilitation fact findings, calamity evidence, and wellness policy;
- funding and accounting workflow for monetization and terminal leave;
- agency treatment of a greater law, CBA, or existing benefit;
- special-law, teacher, contractual, COS, and JO profiles; and
- the audit record for every debit, restoration, transfer, monetization, terminal conversion, or forfeiture.

The agency may configure these controls, but it should not configure ordinary VL or SL to expire, charge weekends contrary to MC41, deduct special emergency or rehabilitation leave from ordinary credits, convert a forfeitable statutory benefit to cash without authority, or treat an unverified opening balance as an earned national entitlement.
