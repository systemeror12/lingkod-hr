# Official authorities for partial-month basic salary

**Research question:** How is the basic salary of a monthly-paid Appointed Personnel computed for a partial calendar month, when the Employee assumes duty or separates inside the month? Which points do national authorities settle, and which block [#621][i621] and [#622][i622]?

**As of:** 2026-11-14 (Philippine Standard Time)

**Scope:** Civilian Appointed Personnel in a National Government Agency, as in [native-payroll-component-authorities.md](native-payroll-component-authorities.md). Casual, contractual, part-time, Job Order, and Contract of Service personnel, LGUs, GOCCs, and special-regime populations are excluded. This record states what the sources say. It chooses no product rule. It records decision D7 of [#12][i12] for [#613][i613].

## Decision

**Blocking.** No national authority found fixes how the basic salary of a monthly-paid Employee is prorated for a partial month of service. Four points [#622][i622] needs are unsettled: the divisor, the paid days, the first and last paid day, and rounding. No source read says whether a mid-month start and a mid-month separation follow one rule.

Until a rule is found, [#621][i621] keeps its blocking exception and creates no Payroll Line for such an Employee. [#622][i622] cannot be built from this record. The one route the sources name is [BC No. 2019-1, sec. 6.0](#sources-used): a case the circular does not cover is referred to the DBM for resolution.

The national rules that exist are for other cases:

- 22 working days is the divisor for the daily wage rate and for the leave-without-pay formula (R1, R3, R6, R7).
- An appointment's effectivity is its issuance date. Actual assumption to duty is a separate fact (R9, R10).
- A new hire is paid at Step 1 (R4).

None of them states a partial-month rule.

## Marks

- **National:** a source states the rule.
- **Partial:** a source states the rule for another case. It is not applied here.
- **No authority found (blocks #622):** no source read states the point.

## Points #622 needs

| Point | What the sources say | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| Divisor: 22, working days in the actual month, 30, calendar days, or other | 22 is the national divisor for the daily wage rate (R1, R3), the leave-without-pay formula (R7), and leave monetization and terminal leave (R8). The LWOP formula used calendar days in the 1999 text quoted by Resolution No. 1400454 until 2014 (R7 note). No source applies any divisor to a partial month of a monthly-paid Employee. | No authority found (blocks #622) |
| Paid days: working days or calendar days | Not addressed. The 22-day base excludes Saturdays and Sundays (R6). No source says whether a weekday holiday inside the base is paid, or how a partial month is counted. | No authority found (blocks #622) |
| First paid day: appointment effectivity or assumption to duty | An appointment's effectivity is the date of its issuance (R9). Certification of assumption is due within 30 days of assumption (R10). No source ties the first day of salary to either date. | No authority found (blocks #622) |
| Last paid day on separation | Not addressed by any source read. The ORAOHRA separation provisions were not read. Terminal-leave rules (R8) concern leave credits, not salary. | No authority found (blocks #622) |
| Weekends and holidays at the boundary, such as assuming duty on Monday the 3rd when the 1st and 2nd are a weekend | MC 41 sec. 33 is an absence rule for a day before or after a weekend or holiday (R11). It does not govern the first or last day of service. R6 says non-working days keep their salary in the LWOP context. Neither says whether a non-working day inside a partial month is paid. | No authority found (blocks #622) |
| Same rule for a mid-month start and a mid-month separation | Not addressed. | No authority found (blocks #622) |
| Rounding | Not addressed. The only centavo figure in the sources (Resolution No. 1400454: 18/22 × P38,373 = P31,396.09) is an LWOP illustration and states no method. | No authority found (blocks #622) |

R1 and R3 state a divisor for a daily rate. R7 states a divisor for an LWOP month. Neither states how a partial month of a monthly-paid Employee is paid. The shared divisor is not a partial-month rule.

## Analogies rejected

The ticket forbids extending these by analogy. Each is recorded here so it is not used by mistake.

- **Days served ÷ 22 × monthly rate.** The tempting reading of R1, R3, and R6. No source states it.
- **Calendar-day divisor.** The 1999 LWOP text (as quoted in Resolution No. 1400454) used calendar days. It was replaced for LWOP in 2014. It is not a partial-month rule.
- **MC 41 sec. 27 leave-earning table** (one day of leave for every 24 days of service, days 1 to 30) and **sec. 28 fractions of a day**. Both govern leave accrual and leave of absence, not salary.
- **MC 41 sec. 33 weekend and holiday rule.** It governs absences, not the days of service.
- **MC 41 sec. 40 and sec. 24 divisors of 22.** Terminal leave and monetization, not salary.
- **Manual on PCC, ch. 3, sec. 3.9.2.3.2** (PERA of daily or part-time personnel) and **sec. 3.9.6.3.1.5** (pro-rated cash gift). Different benefits, and the amounts shown are superseded.
- **NBC 597 and NBC 601, Annex C examples.** The appointment-date examples (for instance, appointed April 23, 2024 or March 13, 2025) show only the January 1 salary adjustment. They do not prorate.
