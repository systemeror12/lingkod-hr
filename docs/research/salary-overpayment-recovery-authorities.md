# Official authorities for recovering a salary overpayment through payroll

**Research question:** How may an Agency recover a salary overpayment from an Employee: by deduction from later salary, with or without consent, in full or by installment, under which floor and priority, and from an Employee who has no later pay? Which points do national authorities settle, and which block [#648][i648]?

**As of:** 2026-11-15 (Philippine Standard Time)

**Scope:** Civilian Appointed Personnel in a National Government Agency, as in [native-payroll-component-authorities.md](native-payroll-component-authorities.md). LGUs, GOCCs, and special-regime populations are excluded. An overpayment here is a negative Payroll Adjustment: pay that a finalized Payroll Period released and that Payroll's recalculation says was not due ([ADR 0016](../adr/0016-payroll-corrections-are-recalculated-differences.md)). This record states what the sources say. It chooses no product rule. It answers [#661][i661], which [#330][i330] (D11) opened.

## Decision

**Partly settled.** The sources settle that an overpayment must be returned, that an Agency may not take it from later salary on its own order, and that a final and executory decision of the Commission on Audit (COA) is the one route they name for a salary deduction. They also settle the floor and the priority for that deduction in FY 2026, and that retirement benefits are out of reach.

Four points are not settled. Each is [named as blocking](#blocking-as-of-2026-11-15-and-where-to-recheck), with the part of [#648][i648] it blocks:

1. Whether an Employee's written admission and consent, with no COA decision, allows a deduction. It blocks a consent route.
2. The date on which a COA-decreed obligation is "incurred". It blocks the order of a recovery against any other deduction that is not statutory.
3. The installment schedule in force, because the 2017 resolution that amends it could not be read. It blocks any check of a schedule COA approved.
4. The FY 2027 deduction list and floor, which wait on the FY 2027 General Appropriations Act (GAA). It blocks a recovery in a 2027 Payroll Period.

No source read addresses negative net pay, or the use of terminal-leave pay without the Employee's undertaking. Neither is needed for a salary deduction.

Until [#648][i648] is built, a negative Payroll Adjustment stays `OPEN` and reported, and no Payroll Period carries it. The settled rules are in [What #648 may build](#what-648-may-build).

## Marks

- **National:** a source states the rule.
- **Partial:** a source states the rule for another case. It is not applied here.
- **No authority found:** no source read states the point. Where it blocks part of [#648][i648], the mark says so.

## Questions #661 asks

| Must the Employee return an overpayment? | Yes. What is received by mistake with no right to demand it must be returned (R1). The salary circular says the personnel concerned "shall refund any overpayments received" (R2). The Supreme Court applies the same principle to recipients of disallowed pay, with two exceptions it decides case by case (R11). | National |
| May the Agency deduct it from later salary on its own order? | No. The GAA lists the obligations for which a salary deduction "may be allowed", and a debt to the National Government is on the list only "as decreed in a final and executory decision of COA" (R6). The Supreme Court voided an agency memorandum that required a refund through salary deductions because the list did not include it (R7). An auditor's finding alone does not make an Employee a debtor whose salary can be set off (R4). | National |
| Does a COA issuance allow it? | Yes. After a Notice of Disallowance becomes final and executory, COA issues a Notice of Finality of Decision (NFD). If the person liable does not pay, COA issues a COA Order of Execution (COE) that instructs the agency cashier "to withhold the payment of the salaries or any amount due" (R8, R9). | Does it need notice or a demand first? | On the COA route, yes. The NFD is served on the persons liable, and the COE is approved no earlier than five days after the agency head receives the NFD (R8). No source read states a notice rule outside the COA route. | National for the COA route |
| Does the Employee's written consent, with no COA decision, allow it? | Not settled. A debt "admitted by the alleged debtor" is set off by operation of law (R4). | May the Agency hold salary while liability is contested? | COA may direct that salary be withheld on an uncontested audit finding, but not applied to the debt until it is admitted or adjudged (R5). It is annual GAA text. | National for FY 2026 |
| Where does a recovery sit in the priority order? | After the BIR, PhilHealth, GSIS, and HDMF. The remaining obligations are satisfied "in the order in which they were incurred" (R6). No source read says when a COA-decreed obligation is incurred: at the overpayment, at the disallowance, or at finality. | National for the statutory rank. No authority found for the incurred date (blocks #648) |

COA may direct withholding of "any money due such person or his estate" (R3), and the COE covers "any amount due" (R8). GSIS benefits and retirement pay cannot be reached (R12, R13). | National, and outside payroll |

## What #648 may build

These follow from the rules above. Each cites its rule. Nothing here is a product decision.

- **A recovery needs a recorded authority.** A Payroll Period deducts a negative Payroll Adjustment only for an obligation "decreed in a final and executory decision of COA" (R6, R8). An Agency order, an auditor's finding, or a Notice of Disallowance that is not yet final is not enough (R4, R5, R7).
- **COA sets any schedule, on the Employee's request.** Without an approved installment request, the COE covers the whole amount (R8). With one, the monthly amount and count come from COA's approval (R10). Payroll computes no schedule.
- **The floor limits each period's deduction.** A deduction never takes monthly net take-home pay below the floor of the GAA in force for the period: ₱5,000 in FY 2026 (R6, R10).
- **Statutory deductions come first.** BIR, PhilHealth, GSIS, and HDMF obligations are satisfied ahead of a recovery (R6).
- **What a period cannot take stays owed.** The Employee pays a month's shortfall to the agency cashier (R10). A recovery can therefore settle across more than one Payroll Period, and partly outside payroll.
- **An Employee with no later pay has nothing to deduct from.** Collection is by the Employee's payment or undertaking, or by COA's referral (R8, R10). GSIS benefits and retirement pay are never applied (R12, R13). LingkodHR records no cash collection ([#330][i330]).

Two consequences fall on the existing model, and [#648][i648] owns them. [ADR 0016](../adr/0016-payroll-corrections-are-recalculated-differences.md) posts an adjustment in one carrying period, and a recovery may need several. An adjustment that is settled in cash at the cashier has no record in LingkodHR that closes it.

## Worked examples

Each example follows the rules above and stops where a blocking point begins. The pay figures are illustrations, not salary-table values. The tax and contribution treatment of a difference is with #348, #350, and #632, and the examples leave it out.

### A serving Employee

An Employee serves in the Agency. The March 2026 Payroll Period is finalized and paid. In May, Leave withdraws a Monetization Earning Input that March consumed. Payroll opens a Payroll Adjustment, and its recalculation gives a difference of −₱6,000.

1. The Employee owes ₱6,000 (R1) and may pay the agency cashier at any time. LingkodHR does not record that payment ([#330][i330]).
2. The Agency may not deduct the ₱6,000 from the June salary on its own order (R6, R7). The adjustment stays `OPEN` and reported.
3. The COA Auditor issues a Notice of Disallowance for ₱6,000. No appeal is filed, and it becomes final and executory. The NFD is served on the Employee, and the Chief Accountant records a receivable (R8, secs. 22.5 and 22.6).
4. The Employee does not pay. COA issues a COE to the agency head, attention the cashier, no earlier than five days after the agency head received the NFD (R8, sec. 23). The obligation is now one R6 item (b) names.
5. The Employee asks COA to pay in installments. COA approves three monthly payments of ₱2,000 (R10, items 1, 2, and 5). The count is COA's decision. The cap for this amount was six in the 2015 text, and the 2017 amendment was not read.
6. The August period:

   | Line | Amount | Net take-home so far |
   |---|---:|---:|
   | Gross pay | ₱30,000 | ₱30,000 |
   | BIR, GSIS, PhilHealth, and HDMF | −₱4,800 | ₱25,200 |
   | Cooperative loan, incurred in 2024 | −₱18,600 | ₱6,600 |
   | Recovery, ₱2,000 due | −₱1,600 | ₱5,000 |

   The statutory deductions come first, and the 2024 loan was incurred before the overpayment (R6). The full ₱2,000 would leave ₱4,600, so the period takes ₱1,600 and stops at the ₱5,000 floor (R6). The Employee pays the ₱400 shortfall to the agency cashier within five days after the due date (R10, item 3).
7. The loan ends in August. The September and October periods each deduct ₱2,000.

Payroll deducts ₱5,600 across three periods, and ₱400 is paid in cash.

### A separated Employee

An Employee resigns effective 2026-04-30. The April 2026 Payroll Period is finalized and paid. In June, Leave withdraws a Monetization Earning Input that April consumed. Payroll opens a Payroll Adjustment, and its recalculation gives a difference of −₱9,000.

1. The former Employee owes ₱9,000 (R1).
2. No later salary exists, so no Payroll Period deducts it. The adjustment stays `OPEN` and reported.
3. The Agency demands payment. The former Employee may pay the agency cashier, or may execute an undertaking that applies money the Agency still owes them, such as unpaid salary or the money value of accrued leave credits (R10, item 4).
4. If the debt is neither paid nor admitted, the Agency may not set it off on an auditor's finding (R4). COA may disallow the payment. Once the decision is final, the COE reaches "any amount due" from the Agency (R8).
5. GSIS benefits and retirement pay are not applied in any case (R12, R13). Retirement benefits are released within the periods RA No. 10154 sets (R14).
6. A balance that money due does not cover is paid to the agency cashier within fifteen days of demand (R10, item 4). If it is not, COA's General Counsel may refer the COE to the Solicitor General (R8, sec. 23.4).

None of these steps is a Payroll Line. Terminal-leave payment is outside the MVP (LEA-008), and LingkodHR records no cash collection ([#330][i330]).

## Readings not adopted

Each is recorded here so it is not used by mistake.

- **An Agency head's power of supervision and control is enough.** The concurring opinion in R7 argues it. The Court's ruling voided the memorandum.
- **Written consent is enough.** R4 supports it for an admitted debt, and R7's concurring opinion states it. R6 does not list it, and the memorandum voided in R7 carried a conforme form. It is a blocking point, not a rule.
- **The Labor Code's wage-deduction rules.** They govern private employment. They were not read for this scope.
- **Civil Code art. 1706** ("Withholding of the wages, except for a debt due, shall not be made by the employer") and **art. 1708.** They sit in the Code's chapter on contracts of labor. No source read applies them to Appointed Personnel.
- **R5 as a hold-then-apply rule for overpayments.** It concerns an accountable officer's cash shortage on COA's direction. LingkodHR has no withheld-but-not-applied pay.
- **The installment caps of R10 as a rule Payroll computes.** They cap what COA may approve on request, and the 2017 amendment was not read.
- **Law-firm commentary** that names a 20% or 50% deduction cap or a notice period for government employees. The commentaries disagree, and none cites text that could be opened.

## Not examined

[ADR 0014](../adr/0014-consume-payroll-sources-by-cutoff.md) lets a late Attendance Payroll Fact or Payroll Input reduce a later period's pay in full. Whether that reduction is a recovery in the sense of these sources was not examined, and no source read addresses it.
